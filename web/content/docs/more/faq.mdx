## Is this legal advice?

No. Fineprint checks that a claim matches the clause it cites. It does not say whether the terms are fair, legal or complete.

## What if the text was pasted wrong?

Register the correct text. It gets a new id; old summaries keep pointing at the text they were written against, which stays public for comparison.

## Who gets the bond?

It stays in the contract. An upheld challenge pays the challenger 20% of the original bond on top of their stake, a retracted claim pays 10%, and a rejected challenge pays its stake to the author. When the window ends clean, what is left returns to the author.

## Can the author challenge their own claim?

Yes, and nothing stops it. It only moves money from the bond to the author's own balance, at the cost of fees, and the clause still decides the label.

## Why is a shorter claim called MISSTATES?

A shorter claim can be FAITHFUL if nothing important is lost. A claim that drops a condition (a deadline, a limit, "without notice") is not, and the judge is told so. If a label looks wrong, the document, the claim and the prompt are all public and can be argued with. The [evaluation](/docs/more/evaluation) publishes the cases where the validators and the spec disagree.

## Which network is it on?

GenLayer Studio Next, chain 61997. See [Addresses](/docs/reference/addresses).

## Why did my transaction say ACCEPTED but nothing happened?

Validators can agree that a refusal is the correct result. Success is an accepted status **and** a leader execution that returned. The site shows the contract's own sentence when it refuses; see [Errors](/docs/reference/errors).

## Why can't I challenge?

The window is over, the claim already has a challenge waiting, or it was already upheld or struck. The page says which.
