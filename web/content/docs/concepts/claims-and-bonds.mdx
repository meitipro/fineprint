---
title: Claims and bonds
description: What a claim is, what a bond is for, and what a challenge costs.
---

A **summary** has 3 to 8 **claims**. Each claim cites exactly one clause number and says what it means in at most 200 characters, in any language. A claim is meant to be a sentence in your own words, not a copy of the clause.

## The bond

The author posts a bond of at least 10 GEN with the summary and chooses a **challenge window** of 24 to 168 hours, which starts when the summary is posted. The contract holds the bond.

## The stake

Challenging a claim costs a **stake of exactly one tenth of the bond**. Staking is the price of challenging: a wrong challenge is paid to the author, so challenging every claim hoping one lands is a losing game.

## A challenge

A challenge names one claim and may point at a second clause the claim is said to contradict, with a reason of at most 300 characters. The reason is stored on the page for readers. **It is never given to the judge**, so eloquence cannot move a label.

A claim can have one open challenge at a time. A claim that survived a challenge can be challenged again, because a different second clause can change the reading. A claim that was upheld, or struck by its author, cannot: **a claim can be upheld once**.

## Vague claims

An author who writes "the terms are fair" gets nothing for it. A claim must cite a clause and say what it means, and a claim the clause does not support is UNSUPPORTED.
